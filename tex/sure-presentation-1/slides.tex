\subsection{A Brief Synopsis of Alzheimer's Disease}

\begin{frame}{What Alzheimer's Disease Is}
    Alzheimer's Disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disease characterized by many complex interactions that affect over 50 million people worldwide.
    Many different aspects of the disease are anticipated, but it is not yet known how these factors interact \cite{maji2025mathematical}.

    \begin{figure}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.4\textwidth]{img/ad_diagram_of_factors.png}
        \caption{Various factors of AD from the literature}
        \label{fig:1}
    \end{figure}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Hypotheses Galore}
    Various hypotheses have been proposed in order to understand the underlying mechanisms of AD; where the leading hypothesis for many years has been the \textit{Amyloid Cascade Hypothesis}. However, there has been increasing support in the notion that it is insufficient in giving an effective description and treatment towards AD \cite{kepp2023amyloid}.

    \begin{figure}[htbp]

        \label{fig:f2}
    \end{figure}
    
\end{frame}
\section{Formation of Proposed Model}

\begin{frame}{Formation of a Newly Proposed Model}
    In order to understand these factors and subsequently the behavior of AD, many systems of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) have been proposed related to these hypotheses; but, a few are highlighted in regard to creating a newly proposed dynamical system:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Observing the dynamics between A$\beta$ and $Ca ^{2+}$ \cite{pal2025nonequilibrium}.
        \item Using causal models observing factors of genetic, non-amyloid dependent, and biomarker cascades \cite{petrella2019computational}.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The Proposed Model}
    Due to the feedback loop created by A$\beta$ and $Ca ^{2+}$, along with the interaction between the hyperphosphorylation of $\tau$-protein caused by A$\beta$, a proposed causal model is described by the following system of ODEs:
    \begin{eqnarray}
        \frac{dA _{\beta}}{dt}&=&a _{1}-r _{1}A _{\beta}+\underbrace{a _{2}\frac{Ca}{Ca+\sigma}}_{\text{Holling Type II}}\\
        \frac{dCa}{dt}&=& b _{1}-r _{2}Ca+b _{2}A _{\beta}\\
        \frac{d \tau}{dt}&=&c _{1}-r _{3}\tau+\underbrace{c _{2}A _{\beta}+c _{3}Ca}_{\text{A$\beta$, Ca $^{2+}$ interaction}}\\
        \frac{dN}{dt}&=&d _{1}-k _{4}N+d _{2}\tau\\
        \frac{dC}{dt}&=&e _{1}-k _{5}C+e _{2}NR + e _{3}\tau,
    \end{eqnarray}
    where $r _{n} = k _{n}(1 - \alpha _{n})\delta _{n}$.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Discussion of Proposed Model}
    \only<1>{When analyzing the model, while equilibria are intractable, global asymptotic stability has been determined for the model. To show this, consider the first two uncoupled compartments of the proposed model:
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        \frac{dA _{\beta}}{dt}&=&a _{1}-r _{1}A _{\beta}+a _{2}\frac{Ca}{Ca+\sigma}\\
        \frac{dCa}{dt}&=& b _{1}-r _{2}Ca+b _{2}A _{\beta}
    \end{eqnarray*}}

    \only<2>{These compartments can be shown to be \textit{bounded} by
    \begin{eqnarray*}
        a _{1}-r _{1}A _{\beta}+a _{2}\frac{Ca}{Ca+\sigma}&\leq&a _{1}+a _{2}-r _{1}A _{\beta}<0 \text{ if } A _{\beta} > \frac{a _{1}+ a _{2}}{r _{1}}\\
        b _{1}-r _{2}Ca + b _{2}A _{\beta}&\leq&b _{1}-r _{2}Ca + b _{2}M < 0 \text{ if } Ca > \frac{b _{1}+b _{2}M}{r _{2}}
    \end{eqnarray*}
    The compartments are also \textit{positively invariant}. This is shown by 
    \begin{equation*}
        \left.\frac{dA _{\beta}}{dt}\right]_{A _{\beta}=0}\quad \text{ and } \left.\frac{dCa}{dt}\right]_{Ca=0}
    \end{equation*}
    where each compartment is positive if $Ca>0,A _{\beta}>0$, respectively.}
    
    \only<3>{For showing equilibrium, solve $dA _{\beta}/dt=0$ in terms of $A _{\beta}$. Substituting this into $dCa/dt$, a quadratic is obtained. Using \textit{Descartes' Rule of Signs}, one positive root is determined, and hence a unique positive real equilibrium. 

    Then to show the non-existence of limit cycles, consider $\alpha(A _{\beta},Ca)=1$. It can be shown that
    \begin{equation*}
        \text{div}f=-r _{1}-r _{2}<0.
    \end{equation*}
    Since $\text{div}f<0$, by Bendixson-Dulac there exists no limit cycles.

    Thus, it has been shown that the proposed model has a unique positively invariant global asymptotically stable equilibrium.}
\end{frame}
\section{Discussion}
\begin{frame}{Potential Limitations}
    While global stability has been shown, there are a few drawbacks that have been noticed in regard to the model.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The treatment terms do not seem to provide an accurate representation of the mechanisms of treatment/provide any new insights to literature.
        \item Fairly simplistic; would like to add more biologically descriptive interactions to model.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Future Directions}
    In order to provide a more interesting description of the mechanisms of AD, future directions include:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Potential mass action terms added to the model.
        \item Sensitivity analysis of model.
        \item Optimal control for treatment terms.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
