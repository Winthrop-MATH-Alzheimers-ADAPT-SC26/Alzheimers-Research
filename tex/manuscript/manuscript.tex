\section{Introduction}
Alzheimer's Disease (AD) is characterized as a complex neurodegenerative disease affecting approximately more than 55 million people globally \cite{maji2025mathematical}. 
Symptoms are caused by the degeneration of the central nervous system, and are portrayed by progressive memory loss, difficulty with language, drastic mood swings, among other effects.
It is a disease that causes an extensive burden to those suffering from the disease through disorientation, to relatives in the form of mental taxation, as well as economically; 
projecting an estimated global economic burden of $16.9$ trillion dollars by 2050 \cite{nandi2022global}.
These factors are but a few reasons as to the importance of research in this area, where many scientists have worked to understand the underlying mechanisms of AD.
However, much is still unknown in how AD is caused, as well as what biological processes occur in the brain, making the disease difficult to come to a decisive conclusion.

Many studies have been conducted in regard to understanding Alzheimer's Disease. 
With this, there are a few main hypotheses that are generally considered to be factors in the causation and progression of AD \cite{zhang2024recent}.
The so-called \textit{Amyloid Cascade Hypothesis} is among the most extensively studied in the literature, where it is proposed that a misfolding of $\beta$-amyloid (A$\beta$) protein form
senile plaques. These plaques are hypothezied to be the main driving force in the intracellular deposition of misfolded tau proteins in neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs) and memory loss in the 
progression of Alzheimer's Disease \cite{hardy1992alzheimer}. Growing evidence however, shows that this hypothesis is suggested to be but a small factor in the larger picture of the underlying 
operative mechanisms of the disease \cite{kepp2023amyloid}. This has led researchers to branch into other pathways concerning AD, looking at the interactions between A$\beta$ and tau in an attempt
to further understand the pathogensis of these two factors \cite{zhang2021interaction}. Some studies have also observed the role of Calcium in this process, further suggesting a more complex process
that is occuring biologically. This occurs through a feedback interaction between an upregulation of Ca$^{2+}$ and the amyloidogenic pathway, remodeling the Ca$^{2+}$ signaling pathway \cite{berridge2010calcium}.

The various complex interactions in AD have shown to be an obstacle to yet overcome when modeling the behavior of the disease. 
Numerous different studies have in turn given an attempt at creating mathematical models
observing different aspects of the disease in order to observe the dynamics in occurance. 
This has led to many models being introduced, each aiming to understand various components in Alzheimer's Disease through different methodologies. 
Compartments concerning the interaction between A$\beta$ and tau are studied, observing the suspected effects on neurodegeneration and cognitive decline through a mechanistic causal model \cite{petrella2019computational,hao2022optimal}. 
The feeback loop between A$\beta$ and Ca$^{2+}$ has also been analyzed using a deterministic system, adding a noise-inducing element to the system \cite{pal2025nonequilibrium,zhang2020mathematical}.
Models that observe lower-level processes have also been studied, lending to valuable insight as to what is suggested to be seen on the cellular level \cite{maji2026quantifying,lang2023stationary}.

It should be noted that in these models, while each has its own way of observing some of the underlying mechanisms, there are still potential shortfalls when it comes to finding effective treatments.
Evidence suggests that focusing solely on anti-amyloid beta treatments or tau protein treatments, rather attempting to determine treatments for many different aspects of the disease \cite{abdulkhaliq2026amyloid,zhang2021interaction}
This provides difficulty in understanding exactly the importance of which component of the disease should be the focus of further study in order to provide evidence for treatments having higher efficacy. 
In this study, a mechanistic model is proposed, analyzing the interaction between A$\beta$, Ca$^{2+}$, and tau proteins; observing the effects of this multifaceted interaction on neurodegeneration, and cognitive decline.
``Sensitivity analysis is performed in order to better understand the importance of each aspect of the model, and optimal control is used in order to determine the suggested avenues of effective treatment.''
\section{Proposed Model}
To understand the role calcium may have in the interaction of AD, a model is proposed using five biomarkers found to have interactions within the disease: hallmark biomarkers through amyloid beta and tau, an interactional biomarker through calcium, suggested to have a role in amyloid beta and tau protein accumulation. 
Also observed are neuronal loss biomarkers, and cognitive decline. 
The pathology of AD is suggested to start through a feedback loop between amyloid beta and calcium through a dysregulation of calcium; this excacerbates the aggregation of amyloid beta, forming plaques. 
These plaques then promotes the hyperphosphorylation of tau proteins, leading to neurodegeneration and subsequently cognitive decline.
\subsection{Amyloid Beta and Calcium Equations}
Because amyloid beta and calcium are suggested to interact in such a way that a dysregulation of calcium causes an acceleration of amyloid beta accumulation \cite{pal2025nonequilibrium}, this is modeled mathematically as 
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dA _{\beta}}{dt}=a _{1} + a _{2}\frac{Ca }{Ca+\sigma} - k _{1}A _{\beta}-u _{1}A _{\beta}\quad \text{with} \quad A _{\beta}(T _{0})=A _{0},
\end{equation}
where $A _{0}$ is the initial condition of amyloid beta at age $T _{0}$, and can vary on a per patient basis \cite{hao2022optimal}. 
Here, $a(Ca/[Ca+\sigma])$ acts as the interaction of calcium on $A _{\beta}$ using a Holling-Type II function.

Similarly, an amyloid beta interaction is observed to have a role in the further dysregulation of calcium, and the equation for calcium is defined as 
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dCa}{dt}= b _{1}+b _{2}A _{\beta}-k _{2}Ca - u _{2}Ca\quad \text{with} \quad Ca(T _{0})=Ca _{0}.
\end{equation}
Here, the accumulation of amyloid beta is positive at a rate $b _{2}$, with a natural removal rate of calcium, $k _{2}$.

\subsection{Tau Equation}
Many studies have suggested that the pathology of tau is initiated by amyloid beta accumulation, and have modeled this process mathematically \cite{zheng2002amyloid, hao2022optimal}. 
However, it has also been suggested that calcium also has an effect inducing the production of tau proteins \cite{guan2021elevating}. 
This leads to a definition of the tau equation to be given by 
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d \tau}{dt}=c _{1}+c _{2}A _{\beta}+c _{3}Ca-k _{3}\tau-u _{3}\tau\quad \text{with} \quad \tau(T _{0})=\tau _{0}.
\end{equation}
It is noted here that only pathological tau is observed, where other studies have used compartments relating to non-amyloid dependent tau accumulation, or suspected non-Alzheimer pathology (SNAP) \cite{petrella2019computational, hao2022optimal}.

\subsection{Neurodegeneration Equation}
When tau is in a pathological state, it no longer binds to microtubules as intended; instead, the formation of NFTs occur in the neurons, leading to neurodegeneration \cite{ballatore2007tau}. Hence, this equation is defined as 
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dN}{dt}=d _{1}+d _{2}\tau-k _{4}N.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Cognitive Decline Equation}
Both tau and neurodegeneration are evidenced to directly affect cognitive decline \cite{bejanin2017tau, hao2022optimal}. Because of this, the equation for cognitive decline is defined as 
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dC}{dt}=e _{1}+e _{2}NR + e _{3}\tau-k _{5}C.
\end{equation}
The parameter $R$ represents a patient having the APOE $\epsilon 4$ gene, which has been shown to be a risk factor in Alzheimer's disease (need to find citation of this).

This model is summarized as a system of ordinary system of differential equations with 




\begin{equation}
\begin{cases}
    \frac{dA _{\beta}}{dt}=a _{1} + a _{2}\frac{Ca }{Ca+\sigma} - k _{1}A _{\beta}-u _{1}A _{\beta}\\

    \frac{dCa}{dt}= b _{1}+b _{2}A _{\beta}-k _{2}Ca - u _{2}Ca\\

    \frac{d \tau}{dt}=c _{1}+c _{2}A _{\beta}+c _{3}Ca-k _{3}\tau-u _{3}\tau\\

    \frac{dN}{dt}=d _{1}+d _{2}\tau-k _{4}N\\

    \frac{dC}{dt}=e _{1}+e _{2}NR + e _{3}\tau-k _{5}C
\end{cases}
\quad
\text{with}
\quad
\begin{cases}
    A _{\beta}(T _{0})=A _{0}\\
    Ca(T _{0})=Ca _{0}\\
    \tau(T _{0})=\tau _{0}\\
    N(T _{0})=N _{0}\\
    C(T _{0})=C _{0}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
